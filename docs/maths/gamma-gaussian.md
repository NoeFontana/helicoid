## 1. The functions Γ_m

**Definition GG.1.** For $m \ge 0$, $\Gamma_m(\varphi) = \sum_{n\ge0}W^n/(n+m)!$ (`NUMERICS.md` §7; CO.4(d)). It converges absolutely for every $\varphi$
($\lVert W^n\rVert = \theta^n$): $\Gamma_m$ is entire.

**Proposition GG.2.** For every $\varphi$ and $m \ge 0$:

- (a) $\Gamma_m = \dfrac{I}{m!} + \sigma_{m+1}(z)\,W + \sigma_{m+2}(z)\,W^2$. Hence $\Gamma_0 = \mathrm{Exp}\,\varphi = I + \frac{\sin\theta}\theta W + aW^2$,
  $\Gamma_1 = I + aW + bW^2 = J_l(\varphi)$, $\Gamma_2 = \frac12I + bW + dW^2$, $\Gamma_3 = \frac16I + dW + \frac{d - 2e}3W^2$.
- (b) $\Gamma_m = \dfrac{I}{m!} + \Gamma_{m+1}W = \dfrac{I}{m!} + W\Gamma_{m+1}$.
- (c) $\Gamma_m = \dfrac1{(m-1)!}\displaystyle\int_0^1(1 - s)^{m-1}\,\mathrm{Exp}(s\varphi)\,\mathrm ds$ for $m \ge 1$.
- (d) $\Gamma_m(-\varphi) = \Gamma_m(\varphi)^\top$, $\Gamma_m(0) = I/m!$, and $\Gamma_mv = \dfrac v{m!} + \sigma_{m+1}\,\varphi\times v + \sigma_{m+2}\,\varphi\times(\varphi\times v)$.

*Proof.* (a) By SO.3, $W^{2j+1} = (-z)^jW$ and $W^{2j+2} = (-z)^jW^2$; splitting $\sum_nW^n/(n+m)!$ by parity gives $I/m!$,
$W\sum_j(-z)^j/(2j+m+1)! = \sigma_{m+1}W$ and $W^2\sum_j(-z)^j/(2j+m+2)! = \sigma_{m+2}W^2$. The named forms are CO.2 ($\sigma_1 = \sin\theta/\theta$,
$\sigma_2 = a$, $\sigma_3 = b$, $\sigma_4 = d$) and CO.4(b) ($e = \frac12(d - 3\sigma_5)$, so $\sigma_5 = (d - 2e)/3$). (b) $W\Gamma_{m+1} = \sum_{n\ge0}W^{n+1}/(n+m+1)!
= \sum_{n\ge1}W^n/(n+m)!$, and $W$ commutes with $\Gamma_{m+1}$. (c) $\int_0^1(1-s)^{m-1}s^n\,\mathrm ds = n!\,(m-1)!/(n+m)!$ (Beta function), applied term by term to
$\mathrm{Exp}(s\varphi) = \sum_ns^nW^n/n!$, which converges uniformly on $[0, 1]$. (d) $W^\top = -W$ and $\sigma_M$ depends on $z$; the last form is (a) on $v$. $\square$

*Consequences.* (i) There is no singular $\theta$ (unlike $J^{-1}$, SO.8): `NUMERICS.md` §12 has no row for $\Gamma_m$, and its only limit is the overflow of $z$ that
$\mathrm{Exp}$ has (CO.15(c)). (ii) The translation column of an $\mathrm{SE}_N(3)$ $\mathrm{Exp}$ is $\Gamma_1\rho$ (SE.3(b)); $\Gamma_2$ is one integration further (GG.4).
(iii) `gamma2_coeffs` $= (b, d) = (\sigma_3, \sigma_4)$; $\Gamma_1$ uses `jr_coeffs` $(a, b)$; $\Gamma_3$ would use $(d, \sigma_5)$ with $\sigma_5 = (d - 2e)/3$ (`q_coeffs`), available as a coefficient but not as an accurate one on the exact arm (GG.3(c));
$m \ge 4$ needs $\sigma_6 = (\frac1{24} - d)/z$, a coefficient outside §4 that cancels by $1/z$ (CO.4(a)). `PHASE5.md` §4 names `gamma1` and `gamma2` only.

## 2. The piecewise-constant increments

**Proposition GG.4.** Assume (A1) an inertial world frame and a body frame with $R = R_{WB}$ obeying $\dot R = R\hat\omega$, $\dot v = Rf + g$, $\dot p = v$, with $g$ a constant vector and $f$ the specific force (the accelerometer output, $f = R^\top(a - g)$ for the free acceleration $a$)
(no Earth rate, no bias, no noise); (A2) $\omega$ and $f$ constant on $[0, \Delta t]$; and define the increments in the frame at the start,
$\Delta R = R_0^\top R(\Delta t)$, $\Delta v = R_0^\top(v(\Delta t) - v_0 - g\Delta t)$, $\Delta p = R_0^\top(p(\Delta t) - p_0 - v_0\Delta t - \frac12g\Delta t^2)$. With $\varphi = \omega\Delta t$, for **every** $\Delta t$:

- (a) $\Delta R = \mathrm{Exp}(\varphi)$, $\Delta v = \Delta t\,\Gamma_1(\varphi)f$, $\Delta p = \Delta t^2\,\Gamma_2(\varphi)f$.
- (b) With $\Omega = \begin{bmatrix}\hat\omega & f & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1\\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ (blocks $3, 1, 1$): $\exp(\Delta t\,\Omega) = \begin{bmatrix}\Delta R & \Delta v & \Delta p\\ 0 & 1 & \Delta t\\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$.
- (c) For consecutive intervals $\Delta t_1$, $\Delta t_2$ the increments of the concatenated interval (the definition above over $[0, \Delta t_1 + \Delta t_2]$) are $\Delta R = \Delta R_1\Delta R_2$, $\Delta v = \Delta v_1 + \Delta R_1\Delta v_2$, $\Delta p = \Delta p_1 + \Delta v_1\Delta t_2 + \Delta R_1\Delta p_2$: the blocks of (b) multiply.

*Proof.* (a) $R(t) = R_0\mathrm{Exp}(t\omega)$ is the solution of $\dot R = R\hat\omega$ through $R_0$, so $\Delta R = \mathrm{Exp}(\varphi)$. Then $\dot v - g = R_0\mathrm{Exp}(t\omega)f$ and, with $\int_0^{\Delta t}t^n/n!\,\mathrm dt =
\Delta t^{n+1}/(n+1)!$ term by term, $\Delta v(\Delta t) = \int_0^{\Delta t}\mathrm{Exp}(t\omega)f\,\mathrm dt = \Delta t\sum_n(\Delta t\hat\omega)^nf/(n+1)! = \Delta t\,\Gamma_1(\varphi)f$. Since
$p(t) - p_0 - v_0t - \frac12gt^2 = R_0\int_0^t\Delta v(s)\,\mathrm ds$ and $\Delta v(s) = \sum_n s^{n+1}\hat\omega^nf/(n+1)!$, integrating gives $\sum_n\Delta t^{n+2}\hat\omega^nf/(n+2)! = \Delta t^2\,\Gamma_2(\varphi)f$.
(b) $Y(t) = \exp(t\Omega)$ solves $\dot Y = Y\Omega$, $Y(0) = I$; its blocks obey $\dot{(\Delta R)} = \Delta R\hat\omega$, $\dot{(\Delta v)} = \Delta R f$, $\dot{(\Delta p)} = \Delta v$, the equations of (a), and the solution is unique. (c) At the end of the first interval $R_1 = R_0\Delta R_1$, $v_1 = v_0 + g\Delta t_1 + R_0\Delta v_1$, $p_1 = p_0 + v_0\Delta t_1 + \frac12g\Delta t_1^2 + R_0\Delta p_1$ (the definitions), and (a) holds from any initial state: with $T = \Delta t_1 + \Delta t_2$, $v(T) - v_0 - gT = R_0\Delta v_1 + R_1\Delta v_2$ and
$p(T) - p_0 - v_0T - \frac12gT^2 = R_0\Delta p_1 + R_0\Delta v_1\Delta t_2 + R_1\Delta p_2$ (the $g$ and $v_0$ terms cancel: $v_1\Delta t_2$ carries $g\Delta t_1\Delta t_2$, the cross term of $\frac12g(\Delta t_1 + \Delta t_2)^2$). Multiply by $R_0^\top$ and use $R_0^\top R_1 = \Delta R_1$; the two $5\times5$ blocks multiply to the same three lines. $\square$

The position column of an $\mathrm{SE}_2(3)$ $\mathrm{Exp}$ is $\Gamma_1\rho_p$ (SE.3(b)), not $\Gamma_2$: $\Gamma_2$ enters because $p$ integrates $v$, and $(\Delta R, \Delta v, \Delta p)$ is $\mathrm{Exp}[\varphi; \Delta t\,f; \Delta t^2\Gamma_1^{-1}\Gamma_2f]$ for $\theta < 2\pi$, not $\mathrm{Exp}[\varphi; \Delta t\,f; \Delta t^2f]$.
At $200^\circ/\mathrm s$ and $200$ Hz to $1$ kHz, $\theta = \lVert\omega\rVert\Delta t$ is $1.7\times10^{-2}$ to $3.5\times10^{-3}$: where the exact arm of $b$ has lost digits in $\Gamma_2$ (GG.3) and the series arm is expected to be the code that runs (the switch point is generated, 0004, and does not exist yet). Earth-rate
terms (the second reference of `NUMERICS.md` §7) violate (A1) and are not derived here; neither `helicoid` nor this page owns the integrator (`0009`).

## 3. The directional Jacobians

**Proposition GG.5.** For every $\varphi$, $v$ and $m \ge 0$, all $\sigma_M$, $\tau_M$ at $z$:

- (a) $\displaystyle\mathcal J_m(\varphi; v) = -\sigma_{m+1}\,\hat v - \sigma_{m+2}\,\big(2W\hat v - \hat vW\big) - 2\tau_{m+3}\,(\varphi\times v)\,\varphi^\top - 2\tau_{m+4}\,\big(\varphi\times(\varphi\times v)\big)\,\varphi^\top$.
- (b) $\mathcal J_m(0; v) = -\hat v/(m+1)!$.
- (c) $m = 1$: $\mathcal J_1(\varphi; \rho) = Q(\rho, \varphi) - [J_l(\varphi)\rho]_\times J_l(\varphi)$ (SE.9(c)), with $\tau_4 = \frac12(b - 2d)$, $\tau_5 = e$: the directional Jacobian of $\Gamma_1$ is `q_coeffs` and one product.
- (d) $m = 2$: the formula needs $\tau_5 = e$ and $\tau_6 = \frac12(\sigma_5 - 4\sigma_6) = \frac1{720} - \frac z{20160} + \dots$, which is not in §4.

*Proof.* Differentiate GG.2(d) with $\mathrm dz = 2\varphi^\top\mathrm d\varphi$ and $\sigma_M' = -\tau_{M+2}$ (CO.13(a)): for a direction $\delta$, $\delta\times v = -\hat v\delta$, $\delta\times(\varphi\times v) = -[\varphi\times v]_\times\delta$,
$\varphi\times(\delta\times v) = -W\hat v\delta$, and $[\varphi\times v]_\times = W\hat v - \hat vW$ (Jacobi). (b) Only the first term survives. (c) Both sides are the derivative of $\varphi \mapsto \Gamma_1(\varphi)\rho$ (SE.3(b)); the coefficients
are CO.4(b). (d) CO.4(a), (b): $\tau_6 = \frac12(\sigma_5 - 4\sigma_6)$, $\sigma_6 = (\frac1{24} - d)/z$. $\square$

So of the two $\Gamma$ of `PHASE5.md` §4, the closed-form directional Jacobian of $\Gamma_1$ already exists in the catalogue; that of $\Gamma_2$ would need a new coefficient (a §4 edit and a record) and is what the ledger row of `PROJECT.md` §5.1 gates on a bench.

## 4. Gaussians on a Lie group

**Definition GG.7.** For $\mu \in G$ and a symmetric positive definite $\Sigma \in \mathbb R^{n\times n}$ in rotation-first coordinates, $\mathcal N_R(\mu, \Sigma)$ is the law of $X = \mu\,\mathrm{Exp}(\xi)$ and $\mathcal N_L(\mu, \Sigma)$ the law of $X = \mathrm{Exp}(\xi)\,\mu$, $\xi \sim \mathcal N(0, \Sigma)$.
The triple $(\mu, \Sigma, \text{side})$ is `Gaussian<S, G, Sd, D>`. If $\theta(\xi) < \pi$, $\xi = X \ominus_R\mu$ (resp. $X \ominus_L\mu$), so $\Sigma$ is the second moment of the residual of that side. The mean is a *reference point* with $\mathbb E\,\xi = 0$, not a Fréchet mean, and the definition is
meaningful for concentrated laws ($3\sigma_\varphi \ll \pi$): the tail beyond $\theta = \pi$ wraps.

**Proposition GG.8 (change of side).** For every $\mu$ and $\Sigma_R$:
(a) $\mathcal N_R(\mu, \Sigma_R) = \mathcal N_L(\mu, \Sigma_L)$ as laws on $G$, **exactly**, for $\Sigma_L = \mathrm{Ad}_\mu\Sigma_R\mathrm{Ad}_\mu^\top$; conversely $\Sigma_R = \mathrm{Ad}_{\mu^{-1}}\Sigma_L\mathrm{Ad}_{\mu^{-1}}^\top$.
(b) For $\theta(\mu^{-1}X) < \pi$: $X \ominus_L\mu = \mathrm{Ad}_\mu\,(X\ominus_R\mu)$ exactly.
(c) For $\mathrm{SE}(3)$, $\mu = (R, t)$, $T = [t]_\times R$, $\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix}A & B^\top\\ B & C\end{bmatrix}$:
$\Sigma_L = \begin{bmatrix}RAR^\top & RAT^\top + RB^\top R^\top\\ \ast & RCR^\top + TAT^\top + RBT^\top + TB^\top R^\top\end{bmatrix}$; for $B = 0$ the translation block is $RCR^\top + [t]_\times(RAR^\top)[t]_\times^\top$.

*Proof.* (a) $\mu\,\mathrm{Exp}(\xi) = \mathrm{Exp}(\mathrm{Ad}_\mu\xi)\,\mu$ for every $\xi$ (LG.4(b)), and a linear image $\mathrm{Ad}_\mu\xi$ of a Gaussian is Gaussian with covariance $\mathrm{Ad}_\mu\Sigma_R\mathrm{Ad}_\mu^\top$; $\mathrm{Ad}_\mu^{-1} = \mathrm{Ad}_{\mu^{-1}}$ (LG.4(b)).
(b) $X\mu^{-1} = \mu\,\mathrm{Exp}(\xi)\mu^{-1} = \mathrm{Exp}(\mathrm{Ad}_\mu\xi)$ and $\theta(\mathrm{Ad}_\mu\xi) = \theta(\xi) < \pi$ (the rotation block of $\mathrm{Ad}_\mu\xi$ is $R\varphi$), so $\mathrm{Log}$ inverts (LG.2(c)). (c) Block multiplication with $\mathrm{Ad}_\mu = \begin{bmatrix}R & 0\\ T & R\end{bmatrix}$ (SE.4). $\square$

So `to_left` and `to_right` have no truncation error to test, only rounding (GG.13); everything approximate in a filter is in GG.9. The lever arm of (c) is the point of GG.13: a rotation covariance $\sigma_\varphi^2$ moves a point at distance $\lVert t\rVert$ by $\sigma_\varphi\lVert t\rVert$. ## 5. Positive definiteness in floating point

**Proposition GG.11 (what the `chol` mask certifies).** Let `chol` (`NUMERICS.md` §15) return the mask set on a symmetric $\Sigma$ of size $n$, $\hat L\hat L^\top = \Sigma + E_\Sigma$, $\lvert E_\Sigma\rvert \le \gamma_{n+1}\lvert\hat L\rvert\lvert\hat L\rvert^\top$ (Thm 10.3, §15.4).
(a) $D$ is defined ($\Sigma_{jj} > 0$), and $\lambda_{\min}(H) > -\dfrac{n\gamma_{n+1}}{1 - n\gamma_{n+1}}$ ($42u$ at $n = 6$): the mask certifies positive definiteness of the *correlation matrix* up to $\approx n^2u$, not of $\Sigma$.
(b) It can be set for an indefinite $\Sigma$, and it can clear on a positive definite one: whether it does is measured to depend on $\lambda_{\min}(H)$ against $u$, **not on $\kappa_2(\Sigma)$**. The bound of (a) and the band of $\lambda_{\min}(H) \approx u$ where the mask flips are the same in every unit; the computed mask is exactly unit-invariant only for powers of $2$, and for another rescaling (rad to degrees, m to mm) it changes only within a small factor of $u$ (measured below).
(c) A clear mask means a computed pivot was not positive, or an entry overflowed (§15.2); the returned $L$ is finite and $d^2$ computed from it is a finite number that means nothing.

*Proof.* (a) The pivots are positive, so $\Sigma_{jj} = d_j + \sum_kL_{jk}^2 > 0$. With $L_D = D^{-1}\hat L$, $E_H = D^{-1}E_\Sigma D^{-1}$: $\lvert E_H\rvert \le \gamma_{n+1}\lvert L_D\rvert\lvert L_D\rvert^\top$, so $\lVert E_H\rVert_2 \le \gamma_{n+1}\lVert L_D\rVert_F^2 = \gamma_{n+1}\operatorname{tr}(H + E_H) \le \gamma_{n+1}n(1 + \lVert E_H\rVert_2)$.
And $H + E_H = L_DL_D^\top \succ 0$, so $\lambda_{\min}(H) > -\lVert E_H\rVert_2$. (b) Thm 10.3 is componentwise, so the bound of (a) is the same for every diagonal scaling of $\Sigma$; a scaling by powers of $2$ rescales every computed entry exactly, any other rounds differently. (c) §15.2. $\square$

**Proposition GG.12 (rounding of $d^2$).** With the mask set and $\delta$ exact, $\hat d^2 = \delta^\top(\Sigma + \Delta)^{-1}\delta\,(1 + \gamma_n)$ with $\lVert D^{-1}\Delta D^{-1}\rVert_2 \le n\gamma_{n+1} + 2n\gamma_n$ ($114u$ at $n = 6$) to first order, hence
$\lvert\hat d^2 - d^2\rvert/d^2 \le c_nu/\lambda_{\min}(H)$ with $c_n u = n\gamma_{n+1} + 2n\gamma_n + \gamma_n$ ($c_6 \approx 120$; $\lambda_{\min}(H) \le 1$): the conditioning of $d^2$ is that of the mask.

*Proof.* Thm 8.5: $(\hat L + F)\hat y = \delta$, $\lvert F\rvert \le \gamma_n\lvert\hat L\rvert$; scale the rows by $D^{-1}$: $(L_D + F_D)\hat y = \delta_D = D^{-1}\delta$, $\lvert F_D\rvert \le \gamma_n\lvert L_D\rvert$. Then $\lVert\hat y\rVert^2 = \delta_D^\top(H + E_H + G)^{-1}\delta_D$ with $G = L_DF_D^\top + F_DL_D^\top + F_DF_D^\top$,
$\lVert F_D\rVert_2 \le \gamma_n\lVert L_D\rVert_F \approx \gamma_n\sqrt n$, $\lVert L_D\rVert_2 \le \sqrt n$ ($\operatorname{tr}H = n$), so $\lVert G\rVert_2 \le 2n\gamma_n$ to first order; the sum of squares adds $\gamma_n$ (no cancellation). For $H \to H + \Delta_H$, $\lvert\delta_D^\top(H^{-1} - (H + \Delta_H)^{-1})\delta_D\rvert \le d^2\lVert\Delta_H\rVert_2/\lambda_{\min}(H)$. $\square$

*What follows for a consumer.* The mask is necessary, not sufficient: with the mask set and $\lambda_{\min}(H) = 10^{-15}$ the bound still allows a relative error $c_6u/\lambda_{\min}(H) \approx 13$ in $d^2$, and a clear mask leaves a finite $d^2$ that is not a statistic (GG.11(c)); a gate is best treated as rejected on a clear mask, not retried after adding jitter to $\Sigma$ (a recommendation, not a specification: `mahalanobis_sq` returns the mask and the decision is the caller's). `chol` does not return $\lambda_{\min}(H)$; its smallest pivot ratio $\min_jL_{jj}^2/\Sigma_{jj}$ is an upper bound of it (the ratio is $1/(H_{1..j}^{-1})_{jj} \ge \lambda_{\min}(H_{1..j}) \ge \lambda_{\min}(H)$, by the Schur complement and interlacing), so a small ratio shows a gate is not to be trusted and a large one certifies nothing (measured $1.3$ to $1450\times\lambda_{\min}(H)$ on 300 random $6\times6$ matrices). A singular $\Sigma$ (a zero-variance gauge or prior direction) is outside GG.7, at $\lambda_{\min}(H) = 0$ and on either side of the mask (`NUMERICS.md` §15.4): remove those directions from the tangent instead of regularising them. `chol` reads the lower triangle (§15.1) and the caller owns symmetry (§15): a $\Sigma_L$ formed as $(\mathrm{Ad}\,\Sigma)\mathrm{Ad}^\top$ is symmetric only to rounding (up to $1.2u$ of its largest entry in the run of GG.13), so what is factored is its lower triangle mirrored; `PHASE3.md` §2 gives `sandwich` as $J\Sigma J^\top$ and does not say whether it symmetrises (an open item in the index).

## 6. Ad at a large translation, and covariances handed across in the wrong convention

**Proposition GG.13.** For $X = (R, t) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$ ($t$ in a length unit, $\varphi$ in rad), with $\mathsf s_\pm = \tfrac12\big(\sqrt{\lVert t\rVert^2 + 4} \pm \lVert t\rVert\big)$:
(a) the singular values of $\mathrm{Ad}_X$ are $\mathsf s_+, \mathsf s_+, 1, 1, \mathsf s_-, \mathsf s_-$, $\mathsf s_+\mathsf s_- = 1$, so $\kappa_2(\mathrm{Ad}_X) = \mathsf s_+^2 = \tfrac12\big(\lVert t\rVert^2 + 2 + \lVert t\rVert\sqrt{\lVert t\rVert^2 + 4}\big) \approx \lVert t\rVert^2$ in that unit (LG.16(c) at $N = 1$; unit-dependent, see *Units*).
(b) For a fixed $\Sigma_R$ and $\lVert t\rVert \to \infty$, the unit-free statement is about the correlation matrix $H_L$: for $\Sigma_R = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_\varphi^2I, \sigma_\rho^2I)$ and $t$ along a coordinate axis, $\lambda_{\min}(H_L) = 1 - \big(1 + \sigma_\rho^2/(\sigma_\varphi^2\lVert t\rVert^2)\big)^{-1/2} \approx \sigma_\rho^2/(2\sigma_\varphi^2\lVert t\rVert^2)$: the correlation between a rotation and a perpendicular translation component tends to $1$. For another direction of $t$ the constant is larger by a factor $\zeta \in [1, 1.2]$ (measured); for a general $\Sigma_R$ the $\lVert t\rVert^{-2}$ is observed (GG.14), not proved. The unit-dependent illustration: $\mathsf s_+^4/\kappa_2(\Sigma_R) \le \kappa_2(\Sigma_L) \le \kappa_2(\Sigma_R)\,\mathsf s_+^4$, so $\kappa_2(\Sigma_L) = \Theta(\lVert t\rVert^4)$.
(c) The round trip $\Sigma_R \to \Sigma_L \to \Sigma_R$ in floating point returns the rotation block to $O(u)$ and the translation block to a relative error, normwise, $\approx c'u\,\lVert t\rVert^2\sigma_\varphi^2/\sigma_\rho^2$, $c' \in [1.3, 2.6]$, where $\sigma_\varphi^2$, $\sigma_\rho^2$ are the largest variances of the two blocks of $\Sigma_R$; componentwise, a diagonal entry of variance $\sigma_{\rho,i}^2$ loses $\lesssim 1.6\,u\,\lVert t\rVert^2\sigma_\varphi^2/\sigma_{\rho,i}^2$ (an entry $25\times$ smaller in variance loses $25\times$ more). Measured on one pose and one diagonal $\Sigma_R$ (GG.14's): first order, not a bound.

*Proof.* (a) $\mathrm{Ad}_X = M\,\mathrm{diag}(R, R)$ with $M = \begin{bmatrix}I & 0\\ [t]_\times & I\end{bmatrix}$ and LG.16(c); $M^\top M$ has characteristic polynomial $(\lambda - 1)^2(\lambda^2 - (\lVert t\rVert^2 + 2)\lambda + 1)^2$ (sympy). (b) For $\kappa_2$: $\Sigma_R \succeq \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_R)I$ gives $\Sigma_L \succeq \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_R)\,\mathrm{Ad}\,\mathrm{Ad}^\top$, whose largest eigenvalue is $\mathsf s_+^2$ by (a); likewise $\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_L) \le \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_R)\,\mathsf s_-^2 = \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_R)/\mathsf s_+^2$, which is the lower bound; the upper bound is the same argument with $\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_L) \le \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_R)\,\mathsf s_+^2$ and $\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_L) \ge \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_R)/\mathsf s_+^2$. For $H_L$, GG.8(c) with $A = \sigma_\varphi^2I$, $B = 0$, $C = \sigma_\rho^2I$ and $RR^\top = I$ gives $\Sigma_L = \begin{bmatrix}\sigma_\varphi^2I & -\sigma_\varphi^2[t]_\times\\ \sigma_\varphi^2[t]_\times & \sigma_\varphi^2(\lVert t\rVert^2I - tt^\top) + \sigma_\rho^2I\end{bmatrix}$, independent of $R$. For $t = \lVert t\rVert e_x$ the $x$ components decouple with correlation $0$, and on the $(y, z)$ components $[e_x]_\times$ is multiplication by $i$ on $\mathbb R^2 \cong \mathbb C$: the remaining $4\times4$ block is the real form (each eigenvalue twice) of the Hermitian $\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_\varphi^2 & -i\sigma_\varphi^2\lVert t\rVert\\ i\sigma_\varphi^2\lVert t\rVert & \sigma_\varphi^2\lVert t\rVert^2 + \sigma_\rho^2\end{bmatrix}$, whose correlation is $\varrho = \big(1 + \sigma_\rho^2/(\sigma_\varphi^2\lVert t\rVert^2)\big)^{-1/2}$, so $H_L$ has the eigenvalues $1, 1$ and $1 \pm \varrho$ twice each. (c) The entries of $\Sigma_L^{\rho\rho}$ are sums of terms of size $\sigma_\varphi^2\lVert t\rVert^2$, each rounded, so they carry $\approx u\sigma_\varphi^2\lVert t\rVert^2$ absolutely; recovering $\Sigma_R^{\rho\rho}$ subtracts those terms again (GG.8(c) backwards), which leaves an absolute error of that size against a block of size $\sigma_\rho^2$ (an entry of size $\sigma_{\rho,i}^2$, componentwise).
The top-left block is $R\Sigma_{\varphi\varphi}R^\top$ back and forth and never meets the large terms. First order, not a bound. $\square$

*Units.* $\kappa_2(\mathrm{Ad}_X)$ and $\kappa_2(\Sigma_L)$ are not unit-free: they mix rad and a length, so they scale with the length unit ($\kappa_2(\mathrm{Ad}) = 10^6$ for $\lVert t\rVert = 10^3$ m and $10^{12}$ for the same pose in mm). A change of length unit is $\Sigma \to D_u\Sigma D_u$, $\mathrm{Ad} \to D_u\mathrm{Ad}D_u^{-1}$ with $D_u = \mathrm{diag}(I, cI)$, hence $\Sigma_L \to D_u\Sigma_LD_u$: the correlation matrix $H_L$ does not change, nor $\lambda_{\min}(H_L)$ and the ratio $\lVert t\rVert\sigma_\varphi/\sigma_\rho$ of (c). Those, not $\kappa_2$, set the mask (GG.11) and the round-trip loss; $\kappa_2$ is an illustration.

**Example GG.14 (seven misread covariances).** $\mu$: $R = \mathrm{Exp}(0.4, -0.7, 0.5)$, $t = (150, -100, 80)$ m ($\lVert t\rVert = 197$ m); $\Sigma_R = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_\varphi^2, \sigma_\rho^2)$ with $\sigma_\varphi = (0.3, 0.3, 1.0)^\circ$, $\sigma_\rho = (0.02, 0.02, 0.10)$ m, rotation first. The error with no counterpart in the type system is the order: `Twist::from_translation_first` converts a tangent, and a covariance needs $\Pi\Sigma\Pi^\top$ (SE.14(d)), which no API item provides.
